# Dehydration

## Overview
Dehydration occurs when the body loses more fluid than it takes in. It can occur with diarrhea, vomiting, fever, heavy sweating, or inadequate fluid intake.

## Possible Symptoms
- Thirst
- Dry mouth
- Weakness
- Dizziness
- Reduced urination
- Fatigue

## Questions to Ask
- How much fluid has the person been able to drink?
- Have they experienced vomiting or diarrhea?
- When did they last urinate?
- Are they dizzy or extremely weak?
- Are they confused or difficult to wake?

## Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical attention if there is:
- Confusion
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- Severe weakness
- Very little or no urination
- Inability to keep fluids down
- Rapidly worsening condition

## General Guidance
If the person is conscious and able to drink safely, maintaining appropriate fluid intake can help prevent worsening dehydration. Severe dehydration requires professional medical attention.

## Safety
The assistant should not attempt to independently manage severe dehydration. This information is for general educational and triage support only.
